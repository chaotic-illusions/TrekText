Research notes
  • thelcars.com uses blink and pulse classes at three speeds, buttons that step through colors on a timer, a data cascade with highlights moving through its columns, and a beep on button press. Its license doesn't allow copying, so these are rebuilt from the ideas, not the code.
  • On TNG most screens were backlit printed panels. The movement came from polarized film with a spinning polarizer behind it, so only chosen areas seemed to shimmer and pulse. Real animation was mostly added in post-production. That's where Flow and Shimmer come from.
  • On DS9 and Voyager, real monitors on set ran animated graphics, most made by scenic artist James Van Over. Picard's screens use real animation on HD monitors throughout.
  • Fan projects such as LCARS for Obsidian, lcars-core and the LCARS SDK add running lights, warp core pulses, radar sweeps, alert conditions with text effects, transporter effects and staged boot sequences. They inspired the meters, scope, power-up and alert condition.

Ideas for later:

  • Power flow: a light pulse that leaves an elbow and runs along the bars attached to it.
  • Exit wipe: when a button links to another page, the screen powers down in reverse order first.
  • Countdown: a readout that counts down to zero, for self-destruct gags.
  • Power-up sound: a soft rising chirp as the page draws on.
  • Cursor: a blinking block at the end of a PADD's text while it waits.
